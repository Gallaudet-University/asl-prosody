Visual Prosody Annotation in a Sign Language Corpus

ASL carries rhythm, emphasis, and emotion through the face, head, body, and timing of signs. We are building a reliable way to annotate these cues, and an open ASL corpus that captures them.

We're hiring annotators Learn more

About the project

Spoken languages mark prosody with pitch and loudness. ASL marks it visually: eye blinks, brow movements, head tilts, body leans, mouth shapes, signing speed, holds, and pauses. These cues carry grammatical, pragmatic, and emotional meaning, and they shape how signers take turns.

Annotated video resources that capture visual prosody at scale are scarce. That limits both linguistic research and the development of sign language technologies. This project closes that gap with a Deaf-centered corpus that reflects the diversity of signers in real-world contexts.

What we annotate

Four categories of visual prosody guide the annotation scheme.

  • Linguistic boundaries

    Where sentences and phrases end, and how signers manage turn-taking in conversation.

  • Non-manual markers

    Brows, eyes, head, mouth, and body movements that carry grammatical meaning.

  • Emotional states

    How signers express affect through face, body, and signing dynamics.

  • Disfluencies

    Pauses, restarts, holds, and other breaks in the flow of signing.

Our approach

  1. Elicit

    Individual and dyadic tasks designed to bring out boundaries, emotion, turn-taking, and disfluency.

  2. Annotate

    ASL-fluent annotators code video independently in ELAN with time-aligned labels.

  3. Measure

    Inter- and intra-annotator agreement is computed with Cohen's kappa, Krippendorff's alpha, and related measures.

  4. Refine

    Blind review and documented consensus turn disagreements into clearer guidelines and video exemplars for the next cycle.

Deaf researchers take part in both the design and the evaluation of the scheme.

Outputs

SignAlign

A Deaf-centered, local-first tool that imports ELAN files, visualizes disagreement across annotators, reports reliability, and supports blind review and consensus.

Demo paper, ACM ASSETS 2026 Posters and Demos, Porto, Portugal.

Annotation scheme

An expanded scheme for non-manual markers in ASL, with versioned label definitions and linked video exemplars.

“Introducing an Expanded Annotation Scheme for Processing Non-Manual Markers in American Sign Language” (manuscript in preparation).

Open ASL corpus

An annotated corpus with usage guidelines, released for non-commercial research and education, plus teaching modules for linguistics and accessibility courses.

Release planned. Check back for updates.
